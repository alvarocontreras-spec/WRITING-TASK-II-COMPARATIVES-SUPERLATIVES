Stage 1: Discover the Patterns

1. Look at the top row (Comparatives). What short word usually appears after short adjectives like faster, easier, or bigger when comparing two things?

2. Look at the bottom row (Superlatives). What word always goes BEFORE words like ______ oldest, ______ best, or ______ most exciting?

💡 Rule Discovered!

  • Comparatives compare 2 items and use THAN after the adjective (e.g., faster than).
  • Superlatives compare 1 item against a whole group and use THE before the adjective (e.g., the oldest).
Stage 2: Why do some words change differently?

Compare "MORE DANGEROUS" vs. "FASTER" in the poster. Why doesn't "dangerous" become "dangerouser"?

💡 Syllable Rule!

Short 1-syllable adjectives (like fast) get -er / -est. Long adjectives with 3+ syllables (like dan-ger-ous) use more / the most instead of changing their ending!
